What we actually do

ReinventOps Group is run by co-founders Uzziah and Arielle Israel. We find the one workflow costing a business the most time or money, put a real number on it, and build the AI and automation that fixes it.

Most teams already know they should be using AI. What's missing is a plan and any proof that it's paying off. That's the gap we close. Every conversation starts with a free 30-minute call, and people leave it knowing whether it's worth pursuing either way.

Who to introduce us to

Two kinds of businesses are a strong fit. They look different from the outside, but the problem underneath is the same.

Companies with teams

Work passes through several hands

  • The team already uses ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, but everyone does it their own way
  • There is no system for it yet, and they know they need one
  • Nobody can say what AI is actually saving them
  • A task repeats daily or weekly across several people, full of copying, re-entering, chasing and waiting
  • Leadership wants results they can measure, not more training
  • Your contact signs off on spending or has real influence over what the company buys: a founder, CEO, COO, head of operations or technology, or the director who owns the process

Owner-run businesses

Everything lands on one desk

  • The owner decides and you can reach them, or you know someone close enough to the owner to sway what they buy
  • Hours disappear every week into lead follow-up, onboarding, client messages, invoicing or content
  • They have been meaning to put a system in place and never get to it
  • Growth is capped because everything runs through them
  • They want it built for them, not a list of tools to go figure out

The 60-second check

Before you send someone over, run through these. Five yeses means send them. Two or three still means message us and ask.

  • They run the business, or have real say in how it runs
  • They can name a task that repeats every week and eats hours
  • That work is mostly copying, chasing, re-entering, waiting or reporting
  • They'd pay to have it fixed if the numbers made sense
  • They're ready to act soon, not just curious about AI

When in doubt, send them anyway with a note. We'd rather tell you it isn't a fit than have you sit on a good one.

Who isn't a fit yet

No hard feelings toward any of these, they're just not where we can help today.

  • Not able or willing to pay for it. This is paid work. If a business can't invest in a build, or doesn't want to, it isn't a fit however good the problem looks.
  • Curious, not committed. They want to learn about AI but have no plans to change anything.
  • Looking for training or a tool list. We fix workflows, we don't run AI workshops.
  • Nothing's broken. Or the problem is one person spending a couple of hours a week.
  • No path to the decision-maker. Your contact can't reach whoever signs.
  • Already mature. They have an in-house AI team getting measurable results.

If you'd rather just talk

Most referrals happen out loud, not over email. Here's what to say.

The 30-second version

So they do AI automation, but not the consulting kind where you just end up with a deck. You tell them what keeps eating everyone's time and they figure out what it's actually costing you. If it's worth fixing, they build it. And the first call's free, so you'd know pretty quick either way.

Three questions that tell you if it's a fit

  • What's the thing your team does every week that everyone groans about?
  • If you could hand one task off tomorrow and never see it again, what would it be?
  • Where do things sit waiting on one person?

What they'll ask you back

Anything in parentheses is a note to you, not something to say out loud.

"What's this going to cost me?"

Depends what they end up building, and the first call doesn't cost anything, so you'd find that out before you commit to anything.

"How long does something like that take?"

Same answer, it depends on what they're building. The more moving pieces, the longer it runs. They'll give you a real timeline once they know what's involved.

"Do I need to be technical for this?"

Nope. You just walk them through how the work happens now. That's the whole ask.

"What if it turns out AI can't help with this?"

Then they'll say so on the call. Worst case you're out half an hour and you know.

"Are you getting something out of this?"

Yes, if it turns into a project. (Just say so. It costs you nothing and people trust you more for it.)
