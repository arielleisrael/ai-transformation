# AI Readiness Assessment Landing Page Feedback

*Review of the qualification-form landing page draft*

| Overall assessment: Strong first draft — approximately 8/10.The design and concept should be preserved. The main opportunity is to sharpen the business-value thesis and make sure every promise is defensible. |
| :---: |

# **Overall Assessment**

**The landing page is strong.** It feels light, intriguing, non-technical, and shareable. Most importantly, it does not feel like a disguised B2B intake form. That is a major win because the experience is supposed to feel like a useful, engaging assessment rather than tedious qualification work.

The recommendation is not to redesign the page. The better move is to preserve what is already working, tighten the positioning, and make sure the claims match what a 12-question assessment can truly support.

# **What Is Working Especially Well**

## **1\. The hero creates curiosity**

**“Your company has an AI identity. Find out what it is.”** is compelling because it creates an open loop without making the visitor feel like they are about to do homework.

**“Discover My AI Identity”** is also a stronger CTA than something generic such as “Start Assessment.”

## **2\. The friction reduction is excellent**

**12 questions · 4 minutes · Instant results · Free**  
Keep this. It makes the assessment feel quick, low-risk, and immediately useful.

## **3\. The mystery identities create an effective open loop**

The four unrevealed identity cards are smart because they communicate, in effect, “One of these is you” without giving away the answer. That supports the gamified feeling and gives the visitor another reason to continue.

## **4\. “Results built to travel” is strategically excellent**

**“Results built to travel.”** may be one of the strongest sections on the page because it directly supports the goal of turning the assessment into an internal conversation starter.

Encouraging prospects to drop the result in Slack, bring it to an all-hands, send it to leadership, or compare it with peers is exactly the behavior the funnel should encourage. The assessment should not only inform the person taking it; it should help that person become an internal messenger for the AI-transformation opportunity.

# **The Biggest Change I Would Make**

**The page currently sells AI identity/readiness more strongly than it sells the underlying business problem.**

Identity is useful for creating curiosity, but the real business thesis is more provocative:

***You might be using AI, but is it actually making the company operate better or improving the bottom line?***

That tension should appear earlier. The current wording around an “AI journey” and a “transformation opportunity” is understandable, but it is somewhat generic and corporate. The page will become more powerful if the visitor quickly understands that the assessment is about the gap between AI usage and economic value.

## **Recommended subheadline direction**

**Discover where your company really stands with AI — where it’s creating value, where it isn’t, and where your biggest opportunity may be to save time, reduce manual work, or improve the bottom line.**

**Core principle:** Identity gets them curious. Economic value gives the assessment teeth.

# **Be Careful With Two Claims**

## **1\. “Your AI Opportunity Score”**

If the copy says the 0–100 score is “calibrated” to company size, AI adoption, and specific workflows, that language may imply a level of scientific validation that the initial assessment does not yet have.

**Safer wording:** A 0–100 score based on your company size, AI adoption, and how work currently gets done across your organization.

This preserves the benefit without making an unnecessary methodological claim.

## **2\. “Your Biggest Workflow Gap”**

The question to ask is whether 12 questions can truly identify the single workflow costing the company the most money. If the assessment cannot support that claim reliably, soften the wording rather than overpromise.

**Possible alternatives:**

* Your Highest-Potential Workflow Opportunity  
* Your Most Likely Workflow Gap

**Suggested description:** Where your answers suggest AI may have the greatest opportunity to reduce manual work, save time, or improve operating leverage.

# **Tighten the “Who It’s For” Section**

The current section is visually strong, and it is reasonable to allow internal champions—not only CEOs—to take and share the assessment. However, the ICP should be slightly more visible in the introductory language.

**Recommended positioning:** Built for leaders and operators at knowledge-work companies trying to turn AI adoption into real business results.

Suggested audience bullets:

* Founders & CEOs  
* C-suite and VP leaders  
* Operations, technology & transformation leaders  
* Department and team leaders influencing how work gets done

This keeps the door open to internal champions while speaking more clearly to the buyer and influencer population the business actually wants.

# **Add a Lightweight Credibility Signal**

The current page explains what the visitor receives, but it does not yet answer why an executive should trust the assessment. A large founder biography is unnecessary and would likely hurt the simplicity of the page. A small credibility line should be enough.

**Possible direction:** Built by practitioners working at the intersection of enterprise transformation, workflow automation, and applied AI.

The final wording should only make claims that Uzziah and Arielle can comfortably substantiate.

# **Add the Market Thesis Explicitly**

**Your company may already be using AI. But is it actually changing how the company works?**

Under that question, briefly explain the distinction between using tools and transforming workflows:

*Using ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, or other AI tools isn’t the same as AI transformation. The real question is whether AI is reducing expensive work, increasing capacity, improving speed, or creating measurable economic value.*

This is the market thesis that explains why the assessment needs to exist. The landing page should not only say, “Find out what type you are.” It should also suggest, “There may be a much bigger gap between AI adoption and AI value than your company realizes.”

That creates the right kind of productive tension for qualified companies to want the next step after receiving their results.

# **Keep the Overall Funnel Exactly as It Is**

**Do not add** pricing, heavy consulting language, a large explanation of the service, a Calendly link, or a long founder story to this landing page.

The visitor should have one job:

**Get curious → Take assessment → Discover identity → Understand implications → Share internally → Qualified prospects encounter the Diagnostic CTA**

The selling should happen after the assessment earns the right to sell. That is much stronger than aggressively introducing the Diagnostic on the landing page.

# **Final Recommendation**

**Keep the design and overall concept. Do not overwork it.** The highest-value revisions are to make the business-value thesis sharper, soften any claims the quiz cannot yet defend, add a lightweight credibility signal, and make sure the assessment result creates the reaction:

***“This describes us. We need to show this to the team.”***

If the actual quiz and results experience deliver on what the landing page promises, this has the potential to become a genuinely strong acquisition asset—not merely a qualification form.
